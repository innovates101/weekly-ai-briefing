Wall Street's New Nightmare: AI Agents That Trigger Bank Runs

Overview

Apollo's chief economist Torsten Slok has identified what may be the most underappreciated systemic risk in banking today: AI agents that autonomously hunt higher-yield alternatives to savings accounts could trigger coordinated, algorithmic deposit outflows that traditional bank-run defenses simply aren't designed to handle. Barron's reports.

The Details

  • Apollo economist Torsten Slok warned that AI agents with authority over user finances could automatically identify and move deposits to higher-yielding instruments
  • Unlike behavioral bank runs, "agentic bank runs" would be algorithmic and near-simultaneous — executing faster than any regulatory or central bank response
  • Banks most exposed: those with large retail deposit bases where customers stayed due to inertia rather than rate competitiveness
  • The threat directly compresses net interest margins as AI agents eliminate the deposit stickiness that has long given banks cheap funding
  • Slok's warning received simultaneous coverage from Bloomberg, Barron's, and the NY Post — signaling serious market attention

Why It Matters

Deposit stickiness has been one of banking's most reliable economic moats — customers simply don't shop for better rates. AI agents that check yields daily and execute transfers automatically will eliminate that friction entirely. Banks sitting on large pools of low-rate savings accounts need to model their funding structure in a world without behavioral inertia.

The regulatory implications are equally pressing. Existing liquidity coverage ratios and deposit insurance frameworks assume human-speed withdrawals. A world where AI agents can sweep deposits across institutions nearly simultaneously demands urgent recalibration. Banks should brief their ALM, risk, and regulatory teams on agentic outflow scenarios now — before agent proliferation makes this a live test.
