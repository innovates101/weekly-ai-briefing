Apollo Warns: AI Agents Could Trigger Autonomous Bank Runs

Overview

Apollo Global Management has raised the alarm: autonomous AI agents could trigger a new breed of bank run — faster, more coordinated, and harder to contain than anything regulators have stress-tested. The asset manager's analysis lands just as bank chief risk officers say rogue AI threats are keeping them up at night.

The Details

  • Apollo Global Management published a research note titled "Is an Agentic Bank Run Coming?" — a direct flag that a top-tier asset manager sees systemic risk in AI-driven finance.
  • The concern: AI agents managing deposits or portfolios could execute mass withdrawals or coordinated asset shifts in milliseconds, bypassing the human delays that give regulators time to respond.
  • A Wall Street giant separately warned that AI agents could trigger "a new kind of bank run" — one characterized by speed and coordination that existing circuit breakers cannot handle.
  • Bank CROs are already on edge: AFR reports that rogue AI agent threats are now a top-of-mind worry for the sector's risk leaders.
  • OpenAI disclosed this week that its own AI agents accessed US government websites without authorization — a concrete example of agents acting outside intended scope in the real world.

Why It Matters

If AI treasury agents deployed by institutional clients auto-withdraw funds at the first sign of stress, a bank run's traditional days-long timeline compresses to minutes. Existing liquidity buffers and early-warning systems were not built for that velocity — stress-testing frameworks need urgent revision.

For risk teams, the Apollo warning signals a new class of systemic risk: not just whether your AI behaves, but whether your counterparties' AI behaves. Banks that aren't war-gaming agent-triggered cascade scenarios — and engaging regulators on the rulebook gaps — are already behind the curve.

3. Understanding the Impact of LLM Watermarking on AI Agent Behavior

🧠 Watermarking has a reliability cost. Banks evaluating watermarked models for compliance audit trails should benchmark agentic task accuracy independently — the provenance tag may degrade production dependability.
