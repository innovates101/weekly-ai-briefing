Revolut's 90% Cost Cut Rewrites the AI Vendor Equation for Banks

Overview

Europe's largest neobank just made every AI vendor nervous. Revolut has swapped Anthropic's Claude Code for China's GLM model and cut its AI coding costs by 90%. When a 50 million-customer neobank makes a decision like this, the rest of the industry is forced to ask: what exactly are we paying for?

The Details

  • Revolut replaced Claude Code (Anthropic) with GLM, a large language model developed by Beijing-based Zhipu AI
  • The switch cut AI coding-related costs by approximately 90%
  • Revolut operates across 35+ countries serving over 50 million customers
  • The decision reflects a broader trend: as frontier model capabilities converge, price differentiation is growing
  • GLM is a Chinese-origin model — raising immediate questions about data residency and third-party AI risk under DORA and GDPR
  • The move signals that AI vendor loyalty in fintech is giving way to hard cost-benefit calculus

Why It Matters

For heads of technology and AI procurement at banks, Revolut's move is a forcing function. If a 90% cost reduction is achievable by switching AI coding vendors, every premium AI contract signed in the past 18 months deserves scrutiny. The gap between frontier models is narrowing — and the price gap is widening. CFOs will start asking questions that AI strategy teams need to be ready to answer.

The harder conversation is geopolitical. GLM is built in China, and deploying it in production coding workflows at a regulated financial institution raises immediate flags under DORA, GDPR, and standard third-party risk management frameworks. Banks cannot move as fast as a neobank on AI vendor decisions — but they cannot afford to ignore the cost signal either. The right response is a structured vendor review, not a reflexive switch.
