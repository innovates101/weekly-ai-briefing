Goldman Sachs' Multi-Model AI Strategy Is the Template Every Bank Has Been Waiting For

Overview

Goldman Sachs offered the most detailed public account yet of how a G-SIB builds enterprise AI at scale — and the architecture is not what most banks have been building. Rather than standardising on a single AI vendor, Goldman routes tasks dynamically across OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google models based on cost, latency, and task type. Details at Goldman’s technology hub. The strategic signal is unambiguous: vendor lock-in is an unacceptable risk at this scale.

The Details

  • The GS AI Platform handles document drafting, competitive analysis, regulatory research, and code generation — routing each task to the best-fit model rather than defaulting to a single provider
  • Compliance and risk teams review thousands of documents monthly using AI, with human escalation triggered by confidence thresholds rather than random sampling
  • Goldman built internal LLM evaluation and red-teaming infrastructure, running adversarial tests before each major model upgrade to validate accuracy and safety
  • Any client-facing output remains subject to mandatory human review — a design principle that satisfies existing model risk management frameworks without requiring new policy
  • Multi-vendor routing also creates pricing leverage: Goldman benchmarks providers continuously and reallocates workloads when capability-to-cost ratios shift

Why It Matters

Multi-model routing is a risk management strategy, not a technical curiosity. Banks that locked into single-vendor AI dependencies in 2024 are discovering the downside: no pricing leverage, no fallback when a model underperforms on a specific task class, and exposure to a single vendor’s reliability and compliance posture. Goldman’s architecture solves all three simultaneously.

The evaluation infrastructure is what most banks are missing. Deploying AI without rigorous internal testing is the hidden risk in enterprise AI programmes. Goldman’s investment in adversarial testing and automated benchmarking is a capability most mid-tier banks haven’t built — and without it, the models in production may be performing materially worse than anyone realises.

2. OCC Publishes AI Guidance Extending SR 11-7 to Agentic and Multi-Step AI Systems
OCC
The OCC released guidance explicitly extending SR 11-7 model risk management principles to agentic AI systems, requiring banks to validate AI agents that chain multiple model calls or execute autonomous actions as composite models with end-to-end documentation.
🧠 Agentic AI now requires composite validation. Banks that deployed multi-step AI agents under single-model validation frameworks are likely non-compliant — an AI model inventory audit should be the immediate priority before the next examination cycle.
