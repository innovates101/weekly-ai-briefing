The Bank of England Wants an AI Trading Kill Switch

Overview

More than half of finance firms already run agentic AI trading systems, and the Bank of England is worried they'll all move the same way at once. Deputy Governor Sarah Breeden is weighing a market-wide 'kill switch' to halt AI-driven trading before a shared signal turns into a stampede.

The Details

  • A Cambridge survey cited by Breeden found 52% of finance firms already run agentic AI systems — faster adoption than the Bank had expected at this stage.
  • The core worry is "herding": AI models trained on similar data can react identically to the same market signal, amplifying volatility beyond what human traders would produce.
  • Breeden said existing frameworks "were not built to contemplate autonomous agents," and that a human in the loop for every agent action "is unlikely to be realistic."
  • Regulators are examining market-wide "kill switches" — automatic trading suspensions extending today's circuit-breakers to detect AI-correlated behavior before it becomes destabilizing.
  • The Bank is running joint simulations with the Bank for International Settlements Innovation Hub and the Bundesbank to study which agent design choices drive herding.

Why It Matters

For banks and asset managers running trading agents, this is a preview of the rulebook you'll be judged against. A blanket kill switch would treat every firm's agents as equally suspect regardless of how carefully yours are governed — raising the incentive to prove your own agents behave well.

Herding risk also cuts across institutions: agents trained on similar data and tuned to similar objectives can synchronize competitors into the same trade at once. Expect examiners to start asking not just whether your agents work, but how differently they behave from everyone else's.
