McKinsey: AI Agents Are Undercutting Banks' Automation Dividends

Overview

McKinsey has fired a direct warning at bank executives: the costs of deploying AI agents are quietly consuming the very savings they were supposed to generate. The analysis flags a structural trap many banks are already walking into — incremental agent deployments funded by savings that haven't materialised yet.

The Details

  • McKinsey finds that siloed, department-by-department agentic AI rollouts drive overhead costs that outpace efficiency gains.
  • Legacy core banking integration is the primary cost driver — connecting agents to decades-old systems is orders of magnitude more expensive than connecting them to cloud-native infrastructure.
  • Banks that automated on legacy RPA face disproportionately high upgrade costs when transitioning to agentic architectures.
  • Governance and monitoring infrastructure for autonomous agents adds a cost layer that most business cases never modelled.
  • McKinsey's prescription: only coordinated, enterprise-wide agentic deployments with centralised cost accounting achieve net-positive automation economics.

Why It Matters

This report should be required reading for every bank CFO approving AI agent project proposals. The pattern McKinsey describes — acquiring automation savings with one hand while spending them with the other — is precisely what happens when agent pilots are greenlit silo by silo, without central ROI tracking. Banks with dozens of active pilots and no enterprise cost model are running a risk they haven't yet priced.

The implication for asset managers and insurers is equally pointed: agentic AI is not a drop-in upgrade to existing automation stacks. Firms that model ROI without accounting for agent governance, inference costs, and legacy plumbing will consistently overpromise. The winners will design enterprise AI architectures — and full total-cost models — before procurement, not after the pilots have already proliferated.
