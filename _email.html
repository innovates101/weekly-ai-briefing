The Agentic Banking Gap: Can Legacy Rails Handle Machine-Led Commerce?

Overview

Finextra Research is asking the question every bank board should be wrestling with: can infrastructure built for human-initiated transactions survive the age of AI-driven commerce and programmable money?

The Details

  • Agentic commerce — where AI agents autonomously initiate, execute, and settle financial transactions — is moving from concept to operational reality across enterprise procurement and treasury functions
  • Tokenized money introduces programmable, event-driven settlement that differs fundamentally from the batch processing and human-authorization models banks were built around
  • Existing banking rails were designed with a human at the point of instruction; they do not natively recognize or validate agent-delegated payment authority
  • KYC, AML, and transaction monitoring frameworks assume a human initiating each action — the compliance gap widens every time an AI agent acts on a customer's behalf
  • Fintechs and neobanks unburdened by legacy core systems are better positioned to build agent-compatible payment infrastructure from scratch

Why It Matters

This is not a distant threat. Enterprises are already deploying AI agents across procurement, expense management, and treasury operations. Without agent-compatible authorization frameworks, every one of those transactions creates compliance gaps that regulators will not tolerate for long — and banks will own the liability.

Payments firms and neobanks have a clear window here. The winners in agentic commerce won't just be those who automate existing human workflows — they'll be those who rebuild the payment layer to be machine-native from the start. Established banks that delay adaptation risk being displaced as the infrastructure layer for the next era of commerce.
