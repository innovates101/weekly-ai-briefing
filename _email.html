AmEx Draws the Line for AI Agents in Commerce

Overview

American Express just moved first — and it matters. The company has published an Intent Intelligence Framework for Trusted Agentic Commerce — a set of principles and technical standards that define how AI agents must verify consumer intent before executing purchases autonomously. This is a card network writing the rules before regulators do.

The Details

  • The framework defines "intent intelligence" as verifying that AI-initiated transactions genuinely reflect a consumer's consented preference — not just an autonomous decision by the agent
  • Targets the emerging authorization gap: AI agents booking travel, managing subscriptions, and executing purchases without explicit per-transaction cardholder approval
  • Proposes standards for auditable consent trails, transaction attribution, and dispute resolution protocols designed specifically for agent-mediated commerce
  • AmEx is positioning itself as an ecosystem standards-setter, inviting partners across banking and payments to adopt the framework
  • The move comes as AI agents are already operating in production across retail, travel, and financial services — the gap between deployment and governance is closing fast

Why It Matters

Payments is the first sector where AI agency creates immediate legal and financial liability. When an agent executes a purchase, the traditional authorization chain — cardholder presents credentials — breaks down entirely. AmEx's framework is a direct response: intent must be verified and auditable. Banks and fintechs processing payments are on notice that intent verification is becoming a standard, not an aspiration. Whoever defines the framework first writes the liability rules for everyone else.

For compliance and risk teams, the concrete question is: can your fraud and dispute workflows handle a transaction where the "customer" is an AI? AmEx is betting that auditable consent trails and traceable authorization chains become table stakes. Banks that wait for regulatory guidance will find themselves building to a standard a card network already wrote — and that is a position no risk function wants to be in.
