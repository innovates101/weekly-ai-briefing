DBS Puts 1,500 Bankers on Agentic AI for Credit Memos

Overview

DBS Bank has deployed agentic AI to 1,500 bankers to draft credit memos — one of commercial lending's most document-intensive, judgment-heavy tasks. Citywire reports the Singapore lender's rollout signals agentic AI crossing from pilot into production at the heart of bank underwriting.

The Details

  • DBS has rolled agentic AI out to 1,500 bankers specifically targeting credit memo drafting, a multi-step workflow synthesising borrower financials, risk factors, and credit history.
  • Credit memos are a regulated artefact in loan approvals — having AI draft them while bankers review represents a meaningful shift in how credit decisions are documented.
  • DBS is Southeast Asia's largest bank by assets and has been among the region's most aggressive institutional AI adopters, making this deployment scale credible rather than aspirational.
  • Coverage across Citywire, Wealth Briefing Asia, and finews.asia suggests a formal announcement rather than an internal pilot leak.
  • The rollout represents an escalation from earlier, narrower DBS AI pilots into core credit origination workflows.

Why It Matters

Credit memo drafting has long been treated as too judgment-sensitive for automation. Deploying agentic AI at 1,500-banker scale — in a regulated institution, adjacent to credit decisions — signals DBS believes the technology clears the reliability bar. Risk and compliance officers at peer banks now face a pointed question: if DBS is comfortable here, why aren't we?

For institutions still in pilot mode, the competitive arithmetic is shifting. Speed-to-credit and cost-per-loan are areas where agentic AI can measurably close the gap. Expect APAC banks to accelerate rollout timelines, and expect regulators to follow with guidance on model risk governance for AI-drafted credit documentation.
