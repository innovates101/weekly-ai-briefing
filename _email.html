Banks Are Racing to Deploy AI Agents Their Customers Don't Trust Yet

Overview

A national survey commissioned by the American Bankers Association finds U.S. consumers are wary of AI agents handling their money and banking credentials. As financial institutions race to deploy agentic AI into customer-facing roles, the demand-side reality is more cautious than the pitch decks suggest—and banks that ignore it do so at their peril.

The Details

  • Morning Consult surveyed Americans nationally on behalf of ABA as companies increasingly offer AI agents for everyday financial tasks
  • Key finding: U.S. consumers are wary of AI agents handling their money and accessing bank account information
  • Bank of America issued guidance the same day warning customers not to share banking passwords with AI assistants
  • A separate report finds consumers are not ready for AI-driven onboarding in financial services
  • The survey arrives as a growing number of fintechs and banks push agentic AI into customer-facing and account management roles

Why It Matters

Banks rushing to deploy agentic AI face a genuine trust gap with their own customers. Pushing AI agents that autonomously access and execute transactions without earning consumer confidence first risks low adoption, customer backlash, and regulatory scrutiny over informed consent and the delegation of financial authority.

The institutions that get this right will treat rollout as a change management challenge as much as a technology one. Progressive consent models—starting with read-only AI assistance and expanding to transactional autonomy only when customers opt in—are likely to outperform banks that push full autonomy on an unprepared user base.
