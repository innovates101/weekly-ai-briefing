Banks' AI Agents Have No Compliance Rulebook Yet

Overview

Financial institutions are racing to deploy AI agents that transact directly with other agents — but as Retail Banker International lays out, compliance teams have no rulebook for it. Risk frameworks built for human decision-makers were never designed for autonomous, machine-to-machine commerce.

The Details

  • Agent-to-agent commerce is happening at scale already, but existing compliance and risk frameworks were built around human-initiated transactions, not autonomous agents negotiating and acting on each other's behalf.
  • Industry commentator Nejc Korosec argues there is no agreed way to verify which agent is acting for whom, or to audit an agent's decisions after the fact.
  • No regulator has published binding rules yet for agent identity, liability, or audit trails — leaving individual banks to improvise their own controls.
  • The piece frames this as urgent: agentic transaction volumes are growing faster than the oversight tooling needed to police them.

Why It Matters

For banks, insurers, and payments firms already piloting agentic workflows, this is a regulatory gap they will own by default until someone fills it. Without agreed standards for agent identity and audit trails, even well-intentioned agent deployments carry outsized legal and reputational risk.

Expect compliance teams — not engineering — to become the real bottleneck on agentic AI rollout. Institutions that build governance and logging into agent deployments now will move faster later, while laggards risk scrutiny only after a high-profile agent failure forces the issue.
