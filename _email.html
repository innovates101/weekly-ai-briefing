Banks Slam the Brakes on Third-Party AI Agent Access

Overview

Two of North America's biggest banks just drew a line in the sand on AI agent access. RBC is blocking personal AI agents from accessing online banking, while Bank of America is warning customers to keep passwords away from their AI agents — a coordinated signal that the agentic era is colliding head-on with institutional risk frameworks.

The Details

  • RBC announced it will block personal AI agents from customer online banking accounts entirely
  • Bank of America warned customers against sharing banking passwords with AI agents, framing it as a security and liability issue
  • A real-world incident has already surfaced: an AI agent posted a CEO's bank details in a company Slack channel, illustrating the live exposure
  • Industry analysis points to a core tension: banks want structured, permissioned API access — not the credential-sharing workarounds customers currently use
  • Agentic AI is already moving into core payments operations at some firms, making the access blockade harder to sustain long-term
  • Multiple major banks are now on record questioning whether blocking is a viable long-term posture as personal AI agent adoption accelerates

Why It Matters

This is the financial sector's defining policy moment for agentic AI. When customers connect AI agents to their bank accounts via shared passwords, they bypass formal authorization frameworks — shifting liability in unpredictable ways and creating audit trails that no compliance team can follow. RBC and BofA aren't being luddites; they're responding to a genuine governance gap that no standard currently fills.

Institutions that move fast to build secure, permissioned API frameworks for third-party agent access will own the narrative — and the customer relationship — in the agentic era. Those that keep blocking will face mounting pressure as AI agents become as ubiquitous as mobile banking apps. The time to architect the solution is now, not after the Wild West is already entrenched.
